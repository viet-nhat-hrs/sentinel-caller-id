This Privacy Policy explains what information the Sentinel Caller ID app ("Sentinel", "the App") accesses, what leaves your device, who receives it, and the choices you have. "We", "us" and "our" mean viet-nhat-hrs, the developer of the App.

1. Summary

  • Sentinel is a caller ID, spam-blocking and phone (dialer) app. To do that job it works with your call log, your contacts, incoming-call events and, if you turn on the Unseen tab, the text of message notifications. This information is processed on your device. We do not operate a server that receives it, and we do not sell it.
  • The App has no account, sign-in or registration.
  • The App includes advertising and measurement software from third parties (Google AdMob and several ad-network partners, Firebase, Adjust, AppsFlyer and the Meta SDK). These services run on your device and may collect device identifiers (such as the Android advertising ID), app-interaction data, crash data and diagnostics, and may show you ads. Section 5 explains exactly what they receive.
  • You control sensitive access (call log, contacts, notification access, default phone app) in your device's system settings, and you can withdraw it at any time.

Default phone app

Sentinel can act as your default phone app. You choose this in a system dialog and can change it at any time in Android Settings. When Sentinel is your default phone app it handles your calls and can use the call log and contacts as described above. These are used for the App's core phone and caller-ID functions only, and are not used for advertising.

Call log and contacts

Your call log and contacts are read from Android's own databases to display and match information inside the App. We do not copy them to a server of ours, and we do not intentionally include them in analytics events or advertising requests.

Notification access and the Unseen tab

Notification access is off by default and is granted only by you in Android's system settings after the App explains why it is needed. When on, the App reads the notifications that supported chat apps post (sender name, message preview, time) so you can preview messages without marking them as seen. These previews are stored only on your device in the App's private storage (up to a limited number of recent messages) and are not sent to us or to any third party. You can turn this off at any time by removing the App's notification access in Android Settings; clearing the App's storage or uninstalling the App deletes the stored previews. The App does not read the notifications of apps it does not support.

Spam number database

The App ships with an offline database of reported spam numbers, loaded from a file inside the App. Checking whether a number looks like spam happens on your device and does not upload the number you are checking. The database is based on the open-source CallShield data set (MIT license).

3. Information we do not collect

  • We do not collect your name, e-mail address, password or any account details: the App has no account.
  • We do not collect your precise or approximate location, photos, videos, files, microphone or camera data, or SMS messages (the App does not request SMS permissions).
  • We do not upload your call log, your contacts, your block list or your Unseen message previews to a server operated by us.

4. How we use information

  • To provide caller ID, call screening, the dialer and the Unseen tab (all on your device).
  • To show advertising and measure its performance.
  • To understand how the App is used, find and fix crashes, and improve stability.
  • To measure which marketing sources lead to installs (attribution).
  • To comply with law and protect our rights.

6. Data storage, backup and retention

  • App data (settings, block list, Unseen previews, local caches) is kept in the App's private storage on your device until you delete it, clear the App's data or uninstall the App.
  • Because the App allows Android's standard backup, your device's backup service may copy some App data to your Google account if you have device backup turned on. This is controlled by your device and Google settings, and we do not have access to those backups.
  • Data held by third-party SDKs follows each provider's own retention rules.
  • Data is encrypted in transit: the App does not allow unencrypted (cleartext) network traffic.
  • Because we do not hold your data on a server, we cannot look up, export or delete it for you. See Section 7.

7. Your choices and rights

  • Permissions: you can grant or revoke call log, contacts, phone, notification and notification-access permissions at any time in Android Settings > Apps > Sentinel Caller ID > Permissions (and Settings > Notifications > Notification access).
  • Default phone app: change it in Android Settings > Apps > Default apps > Phone app.
  • Block list: remove numbers from the block list inside the App.
  • Ads: reset your advertising ID or opt out of ads personalization in your device settings, and use the consent options the App shows where applicable.
  • Delete your data: clear the App's storage or uninstall the App. This removes the data the App holds on your device. To ask a third-party SDK provider about data it holds, use the privacy links in Section 5 or your device's advertising settings.
  • Privacy rights: depending on where you live (for example the EEA, the UK, California), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of personal data, and to lodge a complaint with a data-protection authority. Because we do not keep your data on our own servers, we can only act on identifiers held by us, if any. You can contact us at the address below and we will help or direct you to the right provider.
